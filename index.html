    BUILT FOR THE MUSIC, NOT THE FEED

    A quieter kind of music site.

    FretFree is a small, curated sheet music library and a private notation workspace. No accounts, public uploads, comments, ads, or student work stored on a server. A share link carries a score inside the link itself; nothing is uploaded.

    What is in the library?

    Historical tunes, credited freely licensed editions, public-domain hymn settings, and original CC0 studies. Filter by collection or by the exact edition license. Each entry separates the underlying composition from the modern transcription or arrangement. Mutopia practice parts are upper MIDI-track extracts limited to 32 bars; complete PDFs, MIDI, and editable sources are provided. Open Hymnal parts are extracted upper voices with the complete original ABC retained. O’Neill parts are complete ABC transcriptions.

    Free music comes with different permissions.

    Accepted licenses are Public Domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC BY-NC, CC BY-NC-SA, and GPL. The exact version, contributors, source, and transformation are recorded per edition. Credited editions are not labeled public domain. CC BY requires attribution; CC BY-SA also requires the same license on shared adaptations. Non-commercial (NC) editions are free for lessons, practice and free events, and may not be sold, used at paid events or placed on advertising-supported sites; they are marked on each card and in every export. O’Neill transcriptions use GPL version 2 or later: keep credits, license, change notices, and corresponding editable source when sharing. Export notices retain these details. Original FretFree notation is CC0. Public-domain claims follow the sources’ United States declarations.

    Write, practice, and take it with you.

    Use ABC notation, the note buttons, the notation toolbar above the score, the on-screen piano keys, or a USB MIDI keyboard (in browsers with Web MIDI, such as Chrome and Edge) to write music and chords, with articulations, dynamics and ornaments; dynamics, accents and staccato change the playback. Select some notes and press S for a slur, or add a crescendo, diminuendo or trill line from the toolbar. Press T to turn a note into a triplet, or pick another tuplet from the toolbar, and type letters to fill it; add grace notes with Grace. Press Z or Respell to respell a note, such as C♯ as D♭. Add chord symbols to make a lead sheet; they play as an accompaniment, which the Chords switch turns off. Hear each note as you enter it, play the score with a built-in synthesized sound for each instrument, change tempo, play with a swing feel, loop playback, and export ABC, MIDI, SVG, or MusicXML (for MuseScore, Noteflight, Finale, Sibelius or Dorico). Open MusicXML from those programs too, converted to ABC on your device. Select a passage to copy, cut, paste or repeat it, or to move or lengthen every note in it at once. Measure in the toolbar inserts and deletes bars, and adds double and final bar lines, repeats, 1st and 2nd endings, segno, coda, D.C., D.S. and Fine, rehearsal letters, and changes of time signature, key or clef. Screen readers hear the selected note named with its length, pitch, measure and beat, and ? lists every shortcut, with a search box that runs the command you type. Zoom in for larger notation, or choose how many measures go on each line. Use Print / PDF to produce a paper score, in large print if you zoom in first. Transpose a score or a few measures by any interval or to a new key. Choose a B♭, E♭ or F instrument for written-pitch transposition with concert-pitch playback, and tick Concert pitch to see the notes as they sound. Classroom colors and letters in noteheads help beginners read, and both print. Theme in the header switches to a dark theme, or follows your device; the score stays black on white unless you tick Dark paper, and prints stay black on white. WAV makes an audio file of the score as playback sounds it, made on your device, to hand in or to practice along with. Press L on a note to type lyrics under it, a syllable at a time, with more verses below.

    Privacy and school access

    All editing and saving happens on your device. There is no server. Share link puts the whole score inside the link itself, so a teacher can hand out an assignment, or a student can hand work back, through whatever the school already uses; nothing is uploaded. ✎ Assignment adds a teacher’s instructions and goals to the link, and each student’s copy shows them with a checklist that ticks off as they write. Students press Turn in to send a link or file with their name and the goals they met; the teacher pastes the links into My scores → Submissions, steps through the class with Previous and Next, and sends feedback back in a return link. The share panel also gives embed code for a class website, Google Sites, Canvas or a blog, which shows the score read-only with Play and its credits and saves nothing on the visitor’s device, and a QR code of the link for students to scan from the projector. Library scores and the notation engine ship with the site, so the core experience makes no third-party requests. Local storage can be cleared by the browser or school device policy; export work you want to keep. Unsaved changes are kept as a draft in the browser, so a closed or discarded tab offers them back on the next visit. Saving a changed score keeps the copy it replaces: History in My scores previews, plays and restores earlier versions.

    Offline and installing. After one visit, FretFree opens and works without internet: the library, your saved scores, the editor and playback. A PDF or MIDI file works offline once you have opened it. In Chrome and Edge, Install app at the top of the page adds FretFree to your apps; on an iPad or iPhone, use Share → Add to Home Screen. A new version loads the next time you open FretFree online.

    A school can still block any website or disable browser audio/downloads. This design makes the educational purpose and content easier to review; it does not guarantee access.

    Source editions and credits
